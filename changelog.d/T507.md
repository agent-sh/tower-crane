- A spawn supervisor whose agent exited hands its reactions to a live `wait --follow` host that runs reactions: it queues them (`automation queued` with `handed_to`) and exits, instead of running gates, and other tasks' queued gates, for hours. Without such a host it drains them in a child process as before. The host's controller record carries `reactions: true`.
- A spawn supervisor keeps only its task's events and its task record (`S.taskView`) and makes its state writes in short-lived child processes, so it no longer holds the whole parsed history for its agent's life. On a 116 MB, 135k-line log an idle supervisor measured 88 MB and 0.14 CPU-seconds a minute, down from 288 MB and 0.39.
- Long-lived readers derive history lookups (reaction receipts, the reaction queue, spawned roles, review debts, per-task attempts and exits) once per append instead of on every pass. Between events the `wait --follow` loop measured 0.25 CPU-seconds a minute on that log, down from 4.9.
- `scripts/bench-supervision.js [--compare REF] [--targets]` measures an idle supervisor and the follow loop on that load, median of three one-minute windows, and how long a supervisor takes to exit after its agent. `--warmup-s`, `--log-mb`, `--log-lines` and `--tasks` shrink the load, so `test/bench-supervision.test.js` runs the whole probe, `--profile` included, in seconds.
- A spawn supervisor leaves the host governor's measuring tick, which reads every process on the host, to a `wait` or gate that ticked in the last four seconds, and takes it only when none has. The registry records who ticked (`ticked_by`).
