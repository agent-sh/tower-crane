- `submit --pr` no longer refuses a head whose checks are still running. It records the head, prints the checks still running with a line telling the worker not to wait for CI, and notes them on the task. `check ci` waits for them on the host. Before, workers polled CI with `gh pr checks --watch` or `gh run watch` until it finished, and every poll turn re-read the whole context: T186 spent 199M tokens over 5 attempts, and its last attempt ended in such polls without submitting.
- A failing check still refuses the submit, even while other checks are running, and so does a failing revuto check that no `ci.capped_review` rule matches.
- A hosted `check ci` failure now prints the first failing lines of the first three failing job logs, and the command that reads each other one, as submit's refusal does. The rework reason carries them to the worker. The excerpts pass the redactor the tests, clean and local CI gates use, and a required run that was skipped or neutral prints no log.
- Submit refuses a revuto `[P1]`/`[P2]` finding already on the PR when its path has not changed since the comment and it has no `[resolved]` reply, the rule review ok applies. It does not wait for findings still to come.
- CI whose checks stay queued without starting for 30 minutes now stalls and shows in the inbox, as CI with nothing running already did. A check in progress stalls once it runs past GitHub's 6-hour job limit plus 30 minutes, so long healthy jobs are not stalled. Before, a job no runner picked up or an app run that crashed after it opened was rechecked every minute until GitHub expired it.
- `docs/cli.md`, `docs/state.md` and `docs/automation.md` no longer say there is no CI polling: the `wait` watcher rechecks pending CI once a minute.
- `submit --force-ci-pending` and the brief line `force-ci-pending: allowed` are removed; a pending submit needs neither.
- The worker skill and the worker and researcher roles say to submit without waiting for CI or revuto, and not to poll CI.
