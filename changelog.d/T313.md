- A reader without the lock now also checks that the event log still ends with the last line it read before it keeps a result. Before, recovery cutting a torn tail off the log and the next commit appending the same number of bytes kept the size and the inode, and the reader returned that commit's files without its events. A process that retains its event reader checks the same line on every read of it, and reads the log again when it changed.
- The check reads nothing when the log's times show no change since a read that began at least one timestamp step after the log's last change. On Linux this applies only on local filesystem types; on NFS, SMB, 9p, virtiofs or FUSE, whose times may follow another clock or not move, the line is always compared.
