- The wait lint in `npm run check:shared` now rejects a counted loop that calls a named function whose sleep is not in a loop of its own, even when that sleep carries a `wait-allow`; a loop with no bound in its header that breaks, returns or throws on a counter; and a sleep run through `sh -c` or a shell command string.
- Shell loops in generated scripts are parsed from `do` to their own `done`, so a counted loop that does not sleep no longer flags a later poll on the same line. The rule now also reads loops over real newlines, after `\t` or `\n` escapes, across `+` chains and arrays joined into lines, after a case pattern, `!`, `time` or an interpolation, with `/bin/sleep` or `command sleep`, with a number list ended by a newline, a C-style `for ((...))` header, an escaped backtick `expr`, `sleep $d`, and a counter tested before a `break`.
- The `process.kill` lint flags `const k = global.process.kill` and a `global.process` call right after a `\n` or `\t` escape, and an interposer that changes its pid through `arguments[0]++` or `Object.assign(arguments, ...)` or uses the held alias at the start of a generated script. It no longer reads `calls[pid] = signal` as a pid change.
- The wait lint stops reading a call's arguments at its closing bracket, so the whole-repo check runs about five times faster.
- The live sandbox probes poll for 100 s, leaving margin under a two-minute command limit. The broker stop test names the wait that hangs. The forced stack-sync case releases the held sync 20 s after the second signal, so a regression fails then instead of at the 60 s gh timeout.
