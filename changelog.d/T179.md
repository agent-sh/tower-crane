- A Windows worker's harness starts only after its lease is on disk. The spawn handshakes with its supervisor under the dispatch lock: the supervisor reports ready, the dispatch records the worker's claim and commits it, then the supervisor launches the harness and reports that harness's own pid. A Windows worker spawn holds a lease from the start, so it reserves no slot, and another agent can no longer take the task between dispatch and claim. A supervisor that gets no go within 10 seconds exits without launching, and the spawn exits non-zero with `no lease:`; a launch that fails after the lease is committed rolls the claim back and spends no paused harness's probe interval. Any host without `/bin/sh`, which the POSIX lease gate needs, uses the same handshake.
