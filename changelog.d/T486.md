- On Windows, stopping an agent no longer stops other agents' supervisors. A Windows process keeps its exited parent's pid, and Windows gives that pid to new processes, so a detached supervisor whose dispatch had exited looked like a child of any later process with that pid. A budget, interrupt or stall stop (`taskkill /T`), and a contained agent's exit, then killed that supervisor and its agent, and nothing recorded it: no further live usage, no budget stop, no exit. Tree stops and the containment watchdog now follow a parent link only to a process that started no earlier than its parent, from one process snapshot, and no stop uses `taskkill /T`.
